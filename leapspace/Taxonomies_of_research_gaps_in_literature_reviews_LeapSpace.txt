        Introduction
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Research-gap identification is a core output of literature reviews, but the literature treats “gap” in multiple ways: as missing evidence, poor methods, weak theory, contradictory findings, or underexplored topic areas [1] [2] [3]. Across scoping reviews, mapping reviews, evidence-gap maps, and qualitative reviews, authors repeatedly note inconsistent terminology and limited methodological guidance for identifying and classifying gaps reproducibly [4] [5] [6] [7].

        Foundational Context: What Counts as a “Research Gap”?
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        A foundational distinction in the literature is between 
      
      
        
          
        
        a sparse topic area
      
      
        
         and 
      
      
        
          
        
        a true research gap
      
      
        
        . Frameworks aligned with systematic review and guideline practice define a gap not simply as “few studies,” but as a state in which existing evidence is inadequate for answering a specified question or supporting a decision [2] [11]. This is why many frameworks combine:
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
        a structured description of the question, often via 
      
      
        
          
        
        PICO/PICOS/PCC
      
      
        
         elements, and
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
        a reason why the evidence falls short, such as imprecision, bias, inconsistency, or irrelevance to the target question [2] [3].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        This logic matters for automation. A machine can detect low publication volume relatively easily, but that alone does not establish an evidence gap for review purposes [7] [51] [52]. Inferred gap claims become more defensible when linked to structured review artifacts such as GRADE domains, guideline recommendation status, study-design distributions, or explicit conceptual synthesis failures [16] [19] [38] [41].

        Defining the Major Gap Types
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        Evidence gaps
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        An evidence gap is best defined as a situation where the available evidence is inadequate for reliable inference or recommendation, rather than merely a topic with low publication counts [2] [10]. The literature operationalizes this through reasons such as insufficient or imprecise information, biased information, inconsistency or unknown consistency, and information that does not match the decision question [2].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Guideline-oriented work adds practical signals: no recommendation can be made, only consensus recommendations are possible, long-term outcomes are missing, tradeoffs cannot be assessed, or relevant evidence exists but does not answer the specific question posed [10] [11]. Evidence maps may show sparse coverage, but low volume alone is not treated as sufficient to justify the evidence-gap label [7] [51].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        Methodological gaps
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        A methodological gap is a deficiency in the design architecture of a literature base: absent study types, weak sampling, limited settings, poor measurement validity, insufficient analytic transparency, or inadequate controls for bias and confounding [12] [13] [14] [15]. Reviews often infer such gaps from distributions of methods, for example when cross-sectional studies dominate and longitudinal, experimental, mixed-methods, or context-sensitive designs are rare [16] [61] [62].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        The literature also distinguishes true methodological gaps from mere reporting flaws. Missing protocol registration or incomplete PRISMA items are generally treated as reporting weaknesses, whereas the absence of needed study designs or underrepresentation of key populations/settings is treated as a substantive methodological gap [63] [64] [65].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        Theoretical gaps
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        A theoretical gap exists when a body of literature lacks critical constructs, coherent conceptual integration, explanatory models, or theory development sufficient to move beyond descriptive summary [17] [18] [19] [20]. Narrative and conceptual review methods identify these gaps by interrogating theory use, mapping relationships between concepts, and asking whether the synthesis can generate, refine, extend, or reconceptualize theory [50] [66] [67] [68].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        A field is theoretically thin when terms are fragmented, underlying assumptions are not aligned, conceptual distinctions remain unclear, or the review cannot derive an explanatory framework from the literature [5] [69] [70] [71].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        Contradictory-evidence gaps
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        A contradictory-evidence gap arises when findings, claims, or assumptions conflict, or when the consistency of evidence is unknown [21] [72] [73]. Importantly, the literature warns that apparent contradiction may reflect methodological heterogeneity, population differences, context dependence, or quality variation rather than direct opposition [22] [23] [33] [74].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Realist and comparative synthesis approaches therefore treat contradiction as something to be explained through context-mechanism-outcome reasoning rather than simply counted as disagreement [23] [75] [76].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
          
        
        Confidence level: High
      
      
        
         - The four-way definitional synthesis is strongly supported across multiple review-method strands, even though no single canonical taxonomy unifies them [2] [12] [17] [21].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        Operationalizing Gap Detection in Review Practice
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Review practice makes abstract gaps actionable by linking them to structured extraction and synthesis routines [2] [77]. Common operational devices include:
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        PICO/PICOS/PCC-based characterization
      
      
        
        , which identifies what population, intervention, comparison, outcomes, or settings are missing or mismatched [78] [79] [80].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        GRADE domains
      
      
        
        , which encode evidence shortfalls through risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias [38] [41] [53].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Recommendation status
      
      
        
        , where no recommendation or consensus-only recommendation can indicate an evidence gap [10] [81].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Study-distribution analysis
      
      
        
        , where underrepresentation of longitudinal, experimental, qualitative, or mixed-methods designs signals methodological gaps [16] [62] [82].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Narrative/conceptual synthesis
      
      
        
        , where inability to connect constructs into a coherent explanatory model signals theoretical gaps [18] [19] [50].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Conflict reconciliation workflows
      
      
        
        , especially in realist review, which document how disagreement is tested against contextual explanations [23] [33] [83].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        This pattern shows why structured review frameworks matter for computational detection: they externalize clues that would otherwise remain implicit [3] [10] [84].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        Computational and AI/LLM-Based Gap Detection
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        NLP, topic modeling, and clustering systems
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Several systems identify candidate research gaps by mining topical structure. GapSpotter combines semantic clustering with LLM components and labels gaps as methodological, theoretical, data-based, or people-based [24]. BERTopic-based work on COVID-19 identified 21 gap areas within six broader topics [25]. Other methods use co-occurrence, embeddings, or double-threshold subgraph extraction to find low-occurrence, semantically peripheral, or underdeveloped topic areas [26] [44] [85].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        These systems are useful for surfacing candidate areas, but they generally operationalize “gap” as topical thinness, peripheral semantic position, or future-direction language rather than as a validated literature-review taxonomy [25] [44] [47].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        Citation-network and bibliometric approaches
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Scientometric and bibliometric studies infer gaps from publication patterns, missing citations between related papers, co-word maps, thematic clusters, and topic evolution [36] [86] [87] [88]. These approaches can reveal barriers to knowledge flow or underexplored fronts, but the retrieved evidence does not show formal validation against evidence/methodological/theoretical/contradictory gap labels [36] [52] [89].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        LLM and agentic synthesis pipelines
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        LLM-based systems increasingly participate in retrieval, reranking, summarization, and synthesis. Some use lightweight RAG plus orchestration agents for grounding and final generation [27]. Broader guidance emphasizes iterative refinement, expert curation, corrective retrieval, and human validation to reduce hallucination and overconfidence [42] [46].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        However, the same literature notes that current LLMs are strongest in search, screening, and extraction, and remain weak in methodological-quality assessment, certainty-of-evidence evaluation, and stable cross-document reasoning [90] [91] [92].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        Benchmarking and evaluation status
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        A major weakness is evaluation. The retrieved studies do 
      
      
        
          
        
        not
      
      
        
         report manually annotated corpora for evidence, methodological, theoretical, and contradictory-evidence gaps with inter-annotator agreement and per-class scores [28] [37]. Evidence-gap maps likewise do not appear to validate their gap labels against manually coded judgments of evidence insufficiency [43] [93] [94].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        So the answer to whether these taxonomies have been operationalized automatically is: 
      
      
        
          
        
        partially and indirectly, yes; fully and benchmarkedly, no
      
      
        
         [24] [27] [28].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
          
        
        Confidence level: High
      
      
        
         - Strong evidence supports the existence of assistive computational systems and the lack of taxonomy-aware benchmarking [24] [25] [27] [28].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        Which Gap Types Are Hardest to Detect Automatically?
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        Hardest: theoretical gaps
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Theoretical gaps are the hardest class because they are often 
      
      
        
          
        
        implicit, interpretive, and relational
      
      
        
        . The literature explicitly argues that gaps are not simply “found” but constructed by creatively mapping, classifying, and contrasting literature [29] [30]. Detecting a missing explanatory linkage or underdeveloped concept requires comparing assumptions and conceptual structures across papers, not just extracting explicit phrases [48] [50].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Keyword-driven and lexicon-based approaches are therefore weak here, because they miss latent conceptual absences and hidden linkages [30] [95] [96].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        Also very hard: contradictory-evidence gaps
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Contradiction detection is also very difficult because systems must align claims across studies, recognize stance, incorporate context, and distinguish genuine contradiction from heterogeneity or poor study quality [23] [31] [32] [33]. LLM and stance-detection work reports weaknesses on unseen topics, implicit cues, noisy evidence, and cross-context disagreement [34] [97] [98] [99] [100].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        In review methodology, contradiction often requires explanation rather than binary labeling, which raises the bar further for automation [23] [75].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        Intermediate difficulty: methodological gaps
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Methodological gaps are easier than theoretical gaps because designs, samples, measurement issues, and analytic procedures often leave textual or metadata traces [12] [15] [101]. But they are still error-prone when reporting is incomplete, terminology varies, or key design features are implicit in tables or full text rather than abstracts [15] [101] [102].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        Easiest: evidence gaps
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Evidence gaps are the most detectable when explicit review structure exists. Systems can leverage recommendation status, GRADE domains, PICO missingness, target-population specificity, and future-research statements [3] [10] [38] [39]. 
      
      
        
          
        
        Taken together, these findings suggest
      
      
        
         that evidence gaps benefit most from structured supervision already embedded in evidence-synthesis practice [2] [3] [53].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
          
        
        Confidence level: High
      
      
        
         - The ranking theoretical/contradictory > methodological > evidence is repeatedly supported across method and automation literatures [10] [29] [30] [31] [32].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        Implications and Synthesis
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        The literature supports five practical conclusions.
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Taxonomy use is fragmented.
      
      
        
         Review traditions mix question framing, evidence insufficiency, and topical undercoverage under the same word “gap,” which hinders reproducibility [1] [5] [6].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Robinson-style evidence-shortfall logic is the most operationally precise recovered here.
      
      
        
         It maps cleanly onto evidence-synthesis artifacts such as inconsistency, imprecision, indirectness, and bias [2] [41] [53].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        The four target gap families are still useful as a practical crosswalk.
      
      
        
         Even without one universal taxonomy, evidence, methodological, theoretical, and contradictory-evidence gaps recur across the literature [2] [12] [17] [21].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Current AI mostly detects proxies, not canonical gap types directly.
      
      
        
         Topic sparsity, cluster peripherality, missing citations, and future-work language can help surface leads, but they are not equivalent to taxonomy-aware classification [25] [36] [44].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Hybrid human-AI workflows are more realistic than full automation.
      
      
        
         Automation is strongest for structured evidence and method signals, while human or carefully grounded LLM deliberation remains necessary for theoretical and contradiction-sensitive cases [30] [38] [42] [46].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        A sensible future architecture, 
      
      
        
          
        
        Inferred:
      
      
        
         with low confidence, would be a layered system: first detect explicit evidence and methodological signals from PICO/GRADE/reporting structures, then send candidate theoretical or contradiction cases to retrieval-backed human/LLM review for claim alignment and conceptual adjudication [3] [38] [46]. This is an inference from the pattern of strengths and weaknesses, not a directly validated system [28] [30].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
          
        
        Overall conclusion confidence: High
      
      
        
         - The evidence strongly supports the main synthesis: taxonomy fragmentation persists; Robinson-style evidence-shortfall categories are the clearest recoverable framework; current computational systems are assistive rather than fully taxonomy-aware; and theoretical plus contradictory-evidence gaps are the hardest classes for automation [2] [24] [28] [29] [32].
