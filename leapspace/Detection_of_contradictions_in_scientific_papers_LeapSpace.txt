        Full Report
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        1. Introduction
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Detecting contradictory findings across papers is harder than pairwise NLI because scientific disagreement is rarely just a matter of one sentence entailing or refuting another. The task usually requires finding relevant evidence, aligning what is being compared, interpreting study context, and then deciding whether any remaining opposition is a true contradiction or a consequence of different methods, populations, or outcomes [3] [29] [41]. Current systems therefore tend to operate as 
      
      
        
          
        
        evidence-synthesis pipelines
      
      
        
         rather than as pure text-pair classifiers [1] [2] [56].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Taken together, the literature suggests that the field contains a persistent tension between 
      
      
        
          
        
        semantic contradiction
      
      
        
         and 
      
      
        
          
        
        methodological disagreement
      
      
        
        . NLP systems often frame contradiction as support-versus-refute classification over retrieved evidence, while evidence-synthesis methods insist that apparent conflict must first survive comparability and heterogeneity checks [40] [43] [57]. This pattern implies that contradiction detection across papers is best understood as a hybrid problem spanning information extraction, alignment, inference, and statistical adjudication [10] [17] [36].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
          
        
        Confidence level: High
      
      
        
         - this framing is directly supported across scientific verification, systematic review, and heterogeneity-methods sources [3] [29] [41].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        2. Foundational Context: What Must Be Aligned Before Calling Findings Contradictory
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        A scientific claim can oppose another at several levels: wording, proposition content, entity identity, PICO framing, outcome definition, or effect estimate. Current systems therefore need at least four layers of comparability [10] [17] [30]:
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Claim-level comparability
      
      
        
        : Are the same propositions being compared, or does one sentence contain multiple facts with different truth values? [57] [58]
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Entity and variable comparability
      
      
        
        : Do the papers refer to the same disease, drug, gene state, endpoint, and time frame? [13] [14] [59]
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Study-design comparability
      
      
        
        : Are population, exposure/intervention, comparator, follow-up, and setting similar enough that different results should count as disagreement rather than scope differences? [17] [40]
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Statistical comparability
      
      
        
        : Are effect sizes directly comparable, and is observed variation larger than expected from sampling error? [30] [32]
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        This is why pairwise NLI alone is insufficient. A sentence may textually refute another while the underlying studies are not directly comparable, or two studies may seem textually consistent while effect estimates differ materially once standardized synthesis is attempted [41] [57] [60].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
          
        
        Confidence level: High
      
      
        
         - the multi-level comparability requirement is strongly supported, though not formalized as a single universal framework in the retrieved sources [10] [30] [40].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        3. Claim Normalization Before Comparison
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        3.1 Canonical claim representations
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Current systems often simplify comparison by decomposing long text into smaller meaning units before inference. Examples include proposition-level segmentation, sentence-level retrieval/fusion, and document-to-sentence simplification [57] [58] [61]. In scientific settings, claim-evidence interfaces decompose answers and source documents into discrete claims and evidence units, while ASQ converts query text into 
      
      
        
          
        
        Subject-Predicate-Object
      
      
        
         triples aligned to evidence entities [10] [62]. SemRep and related biomedical extraction systems similarly represent text as subject-relation-object predications rather than raw strings [12] [63].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        The practical purpose is to compare 
      
      
        
          
        
        the same proposition
      
      
        
         rather than entire abstracts containing multiple mixed claims [57] [58]. This matters because different propositions in one sentence may have different entailment status relative to another document [57].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        3.2 Entity normalization and ontology linking
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Biomedical pipelines commonly perform NER followed by entity normalization into controlled vocabularies such as UMLS, MeSH, HGNC, ICD-10, DrugBank, RxNorm, or Entrez [13] [64] [65] [66] [67] [68]. Ontology alignment also appears in triple-based extraction systems, where subjects and objects are attached to canonical identifiers before downstream comparison [10] [12] [69].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        The motivation is not merely vocabulary cleanup. In biomedical text, distinct gene/protein states, modifiers, abbreviations, and orthographic variants can collapse into apparently identical entities if normalization is poor, creating spurious contradiction pairs [14] [70] [71]. Coreference and abbreviation handling are likewise treated as important preprocessing for full-text comparison [11] [72] [73].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        3.3 Benefits and failure modes
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Normalization helps by reducing ambiguity and improving representation quality, and some component papers report improved normalization or NER/NEN metrics after architectural refinements or LLM-assisted reranking [13] [15] [16]. However, the retrieved literature rarely provides direct ablations showing that normalization reduces final cross-paper contradiction errors [14] [50].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Still, the evidence is consistent on the main failure mode: poor normalization can make biologically distinct entities look comparable and thereby inflate false contradiction detections [14].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
          
        
        Confidence level: High
      
      
        
         for normalization as a prerequisite; 
      
      
        
          
        
        Medium
      
      
        
         for quantified downstream gains, because direct contradiction-focused ablations are sparse [10] [14].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        4. PICO and Variable Alignment Across Papers
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        4.1 PICO/PICOS matching
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Evidence-synthesis methods insist that review questions and studies be decomposed into explicit PICO-like structures before results are compared or grouped [17] [18]. GRADE asks whether population, intervention, comparator, and outcomes are sufficiently similar for a similar effect size to be plausible [17] [74]. Structured comparison frameworks such as PICOTS-ComTeC extend this with setting, timing, and other domains; one comparison found 81% domain overlap and 48% subcategory overlap across frameworks [75].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Some workflows assess whether PICO elements are entirely matching, partly matching, or non-matching against a reference question [76]. But across the retrieved literature, explicit partial-vs-full matching taxonomies are more common in review methods and guideline comparison than in automated contradiction systems [75] [76].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        4.2 Outcome and variable harmonization
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Variable and outcome alignment is typically done with a mix of:
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Ontology/rule-based matching
      
      
        
         using lexical, structural, hierarchical, syntactic, axiomatic, and context rules [77] [78].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Lexical and semantic similarity
      
      
        
         rules for synonyms, common substrings, edit distance, and ontology-based equivalence [79] [80] [81].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Embedding-based methods
      
      
        
         such as SBERT/BERT for contextual phrase similarity [19] [55] [82].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        LLM/GPT-based second-stage verification
      
      
        
         for semantically distant or ambiguous mappings [20] [83] [84].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        A concrete threshold is reported in one 2025 ontology-matching workflow: class pairs were validated only when confidence exceeded 
      
      
        
          
        
        0.65
      
      
        
        , after which humans checked semantic correctness [22]. Another medical mapping workflow used UMLS semantic-type filtering and improved agreement with clinical thinking from 
      
      
        
          
        
        68% to 96%
      
      
        
         [23].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        4.3 Handling incomplete structure
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Rigid PICO matching often fails because real studies omit or incompletely report crucial elements [85] [86]. Review workflows may omit some PICO terms to maximize sensitivity, accept relaxed matching, or translate only part of a question into structured form while using free-text predicates to recover omitted aspects [21] [87] [88] [89].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        The main implication is that variable alignment in contradiction detection is still more mature as an 
      
      
        
          
        
        evidence-synthesis discipline
      
      
        
         than as an end-to-end automated classifier [17] [20] [21].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
          
        
        Confidence level: High
      
      
        
         - the alignment methods and thresholds are directly evidenced, though automation of full contradiction adjudication remains incomplete [22] [23].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        5. Retrieval, Reranking, and Stance Detection Pipelines
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        5.1 Retrieval-first design
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Scientific contradiction systems generally use three stages: 
      
      
        
          
        
        document retrieval
      
      
        
        , 
      
      
        
          
        
        evidence or rationale selection
      
      
        
        , and 
      
      
        
          
        
        claim verification
      
      
        
         cast as NLI or stance classification [1] [90] [91]. This staged design appears in FEVER, SCIFACT, HEALTHVER, MULTIVERS, ARSJOINT, and clinical-trial NLI systems [2] [3] [24] [56] [92].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Reranking or evidence refinement is common because error propagation is a recognized bottleneck [25] [56] [93]. Some systems also operate at sentence or proposition level and later fuse evidence-backed subdecisions into a higher-level verdict [26] [58].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        5.2 Support/refute/neutral classification
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Retrieved evidence is then labeled as support, refute, neutral, entailment, contradiction, or related variants depending on the benchmark [2] [3] [24]. Aggregation can be done with pooling, attention, MLP fusion, concatenation, or weighted multi-view combination [26] [94] [95]. Some evaluation work also uses a document-level criterion such as MaxNLL after sentence aggregation [96].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        However, the retrieved sources do 
      
      
        
          
        
        not
      
      
        
         show a single standard paper-level rule for converting multiple evidence stances into a final cross-paper contradiction judgment, nor do they consistently expose confidence cutoffs for that final step [5] [27].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        5.3 Reported performance
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Benchmark performance is best documented for retrieval and NLI subtasks:
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        FEVER
      
      
        
        : 31.87% accuracy when correct evidence must be retrieved versus 50.91% label-only, highlighting retrieval as a major bottleneck [2].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        SemEval clinical-trial NLI
      
      
        
        : Flan-T5 multi-evidence system achieved 
      
      
        
          
        
        0.834 F1
      
      
        
         [28].

        6. Effect-Size Heterogeneity and Contradiction Thresholds
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        6.1 Heterogeneity statistics actually used
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        The clearest numeric thresholds in this literature come from meta-analytic heterogeneity assessment:
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Cochran’s Q
      
      
        
        : significant heterogeneity thresholds reported at 
      
      
        
          
        
        p<0.10
      
      
        
        , and in some studies 
      
      
        
          
        
        p<0.05
      
      
        
         or 
      
      
        
          
        
        p<0.01
      
      
        
         [32] [33] [98] [99] [100].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        I2
      
      
        
        : commonly interpreted using bands around 
      
      
        
          
        
        25% / 50% / 75%
      
      
        
         for low, moderate, and high heterogeneity, with variants such as <25 low, 25-50 moderate, >50 substantial, >75 high [32] [34] [35] [100] [101] [102] [103] [104] [105].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        tau2
      
      
        
        : treated as between-study variance; one paper uses 
      
      
        
          
        
        tau2>1.0
      
      
        
         for substantial heterogeneity [33] [34].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Prediction intervals
      
      
        
        : used to judge how wide the range of true effects may be across settings, though no universal cutoff is given [33] [105] [106].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Model choice is often tied to these thresholds, for example 
      
      
        
          
        
        random-effects when I2>50%
      
      
        
         or when populations/methods plausibly differ [34] [35] [45] [101].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        6.2 Why CI overlap is not enough
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Several sources warn that contradiction should not be judged from CI overlap alone. Two 95% CIs can overlap even when the direct difference is statistically significant, so non-overlap is not required for significance [44] [107] [108]. The recommended approach is to compare the 
      
      
        
          
        
        estimated difference directly
      
      
        
        , with its own CI, rather than separately checking whether each study is “significant” or whether intervals overlap [30] [109] [110].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        This is central to contradiction verification: 
      
      
        
          
        
        statistical non-significance indicates uncertainty or low precision, not necessarily agreement
      
      
        
        , while substantive contradiction requires direct incompatibility of comparable estimates [30] [60].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        6.3 Distinguishing contradiction from heterogeneity
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Evidence-synthesis methods usually ask first whether studies are similar enough to combine, focusing on intervention mechanism, population, follow-up, comparator, and outcome similarity [40]. If not, apparent conflict is treated as heterogeneity or non-comparability rather than contradiction [29] [41]. If heterogeneity is present, the next steps are subgroup analysis, moderator analysis, or meta-regression rather than immediate contradiction labeling [31] [52] [111].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        There are also practical thresholds for follow-up analysis: some sources suggest moderator testing only when there are enough studies, such as at least 
      
      
        
          
        
        4 studies per category
      
      
        
        , at least 
      
      
        
          
        
        5 effect sizes
      
      
        
        , or at least 
      
      
        
          
        
        10 studies
      
      
        
         for some moderator or bias tests [45] [112] [113] [114].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
          
        
        Confidence level: High
      
      
        
         - heterogeneity thresholds are among the most consistently reported quantitative conventions in the supplied evidence [32] [35].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        7. Verification Workflows Before Declaring Genuine Contradiction
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        The most rigorous workflows are still heavily human-guided. Before labeling two findings contradictory, review methods typically require:
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Independent full-text review and data extraction
      
      
        
        , often by two reviewers [36] [115].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Duplicate risk-of-bias assessment
      
      
        
        , with discrepancies resolved by consensus or a third reviewer [39] [116].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Comparison of effect estimates and 95% CIs
      
      
        
         [37] [117].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Heterogeneity assessment
      
      
        
         using Q, I2, tau2, and sometimes prediction intervals [32] [37].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Subgroup and sensitivity analyses
      
      
        
         to test whether differences persist across strata [38] [118].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Publication-bias checks
      
      
        
         such as funnel plots and related tests [38] [119].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Final adjudication
      
      
        
        : if study differences explain the disagreement, the outcome is heterogeneous/inconclusive rather than contradictory [40] [41].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        The supplied evidence does not quantify how often these steps change the final disagreement label, but it does show that duplicate extraction and adjudication are standard recommendations [39] [115].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
          
        
        Confidence level: High
      
      
        
         for workflow steps; 
      
      
        
          
        
        Low
      
      
        
         for estimates of how frequently they alter labels, because those numbers are not provided [115].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        8. Benchmarks, Annotation Schemes, and Accuracy Limits
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Available benchmarks do not yet cleanly operationalize the full distinction between contradiction and methodological heterogeneity. FEVER, SCIFACT, HEALTHVER, and MULTIVERS focus on evidence-backed support/refute labeling, not full cross-paper synthesis adjudication [2] [3] [24] [92]. Biomedical contradiction corpora exist, including a 259-abstract corpus and ES-Contradiction, but the retrieved excerpts do not expose robust annotation protocols for contradiction versus uncertainty, partial overlap, and methodological heterogeneity with full inter-annotator agreement details [42] [43] [120].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Some narrower contradiction datasets report strong results:
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        IsoLex
      
      
        
        : 
      
      
        
          
        
        92% accuracy
      
      
        
         and 
      
      
        
          
        
        94% F1
      
      
        
         on SICK-CE [46].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        ES-Contradiction
      
      
        
        : 
      
      
        
          
        
        92.47% F1
      
      
        
        ; dataset includes Compatible, Contradiction, and Unrelated labels, with contradiction subtypes such as negation, antonyms, numerical, and structural [48] [121].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        But these are not equivalent to real cross-paper scientific contradiction verification because they do not combine retrieval, PICO alignment, effect comparability, and heterogeneity adjudication in one end-to-end task [43] [122].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        A recurring failure mode is that systems confuse genuine contradiction with:
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
        methodological or clinical heterogeneity [41] [123],
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
        extraction/normalization errors [14] [122],
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
        partial-claim support where evidence only covers a special case [124],
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
        and subtle reasoning failures in stance prediction [125].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
          
        
        Confidence level: Medium
      
      
        
         - the benchmark gap is clear, but detailed label protocols are often absent from the retrieved excerpts [42] [43].
      
    
    

        
        
      
      
        
        9. Practical Synthesis: What a Current Best-Practice System Looks Like
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
        Taken together, the evidence supports the following best-practice workflow for contradiction detection across papers:
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Claim decomposition
      
      
        
         into propositions or SPO/triple forms [10] [12].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Entity normalization
      
      
        
         to controlled vocabularies, with abbreviation/coreference handling [11] [13].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        PICO or variable alignment
      
      
        
        , using ontology, lexical, embedding, and LLM-based checks, with relaxed matching where reporting is incomplete [19] [20] [21].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Evidence retrieval and reranking
      
      
        
         over abstracts or sentences [1] [25].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Stance classification
      
      
        
         at evidence level as support/refute/neutral or entailment/contradiction [3] [24].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Effect comparison and heterogeneity gate
      
      
        
        : apply Q, I2, tau2, prediction intervals, direct difference estimation, subgroup/moderator analysis, and design checks before making a contradiction call [30] [32] [45].
      
    
    

        
          
          
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Human adjudication for ambiguous cases
      
      
        
        , especially where semantic refutation coexists with partial PICO mismatch or high heterogeneity [36] [115].
      
    
    

        
      
      
        
          
        
        Inferred:
      
      
        
         a next-generation system would likely benefit from replacing a single contradiction label with categories such as 
      
      
        
          
        
        semantic refutation
      
      
        
        , 
      
      
        
          
        
        estimate-direction conflict
      
      
        
        , 
      
      
        
          
        
        non-comparable methodology
      
      
        
        , and 
      
      
        
          
        
        inconclusive disagreement
      
      
        
         [43] [51].
      
      
        
        
      
      
        
          
        
        Confidence level: Low
      
      
        
         - this taxonomy is a synthesis of the evidence rather than a directly reported standard.

        Current systems go beyond pairwise NLI by reframing contradiction detection as a
